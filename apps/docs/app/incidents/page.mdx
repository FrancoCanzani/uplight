# Incidents

When a monitor fails or a heartbeat is missed, Uplight automatically creates an incident. You can track, manage, and resolve incidents directly in the dashboard.

## How Incidents Work

1. A monitor check fails or a heartbeat is missed
2. Uplight creates an incident automatically
3. Notifications are sent to your configured channels
4. Your team can acknowledge, work on, and resolve the incident
5. The incident timeline captures all activity

## Incident States

Incidents progress through these states:

| State | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Ongoing** | The incident is active and unacknowledged |
| **Acknowledged** | Someone has seen and accepted the incident |
| **Fixing** | The team is actively working on a fix |
| **Recovered** | The service is back up, but incident not yet closed |
| **Resolved** | The incident is closed |

### Typical Workflow

```
Ongoing → Acknowledged → Fixing → Recovered → Resolved
```

You can skip states if needed (e.g., go directly from Ongoing to Resolved for minor issues).

## Severity Levels

Set the severity to communicate impact:

| Level | Description | Typical Use |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Low** | Minor issue, no user impact | Slow response times |
| **Medium** | Some users affected | Partial service degradation |
| **High** | Significant user impact | Major feature unavailable |
| **Critical** | Complete outage | Service completely down |

## Incident Types

Categorize incidents for better organization and reporting:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Availability** | Service is down or unreachable |
| **Performance** | Service is slow or degraded |
| **Security** | Security-related issue |
| **Data** | Data integrity or loss issue |
| **Other** | Doesn't fit other categories |

## Managing Incidents

### Viewing Incidents

Navigate to **Incidents** in the sidebar to see all incidents. You can filter by:

- Status (ongoing, acknowledged, resolved, etc.)
- Severity
- Type
- Monitor

### Acknowledging an Incident

When you start looking at an incident:

1. Open the incident
2. Click **Acknowledge**

This tells your team someone is aware and looking into it.

### Updating Status

As you work on the incident:

1. Open the incident
2. Use the status dropdown to update (e.g., "Fixing")
3. Add a comment explaining what you're doing

### Assigning Incidents

Assign incidents to specific team members:

1. Open the incident
2. Click on the assignee field
3. Select a team member

### Adding Comments

Keep your team informed with comments:

1. Open the incident
2. Scroll to the activity section
3. Type your update
4. Click **Add Comment**

Comments appear in the incident timeline and help with post-mortems.

## Incident Timeline

Every incident has a timeline showing:

- When the incident was created
- Status changes
- Comments and updates
- Who made each change
- When the incident was resolved

This history is invaluable for understanding what happened and improving your response.

## Post-Mortems

After resolving a significant incident, document what happened:

1. Open the resolved incident
2. Go to the **Post-Mortem** section
3. Document:
   - What went wrong
   - How it was detected
   - What was done to fix it
   - How to prevent it in the future

Good post-mortems help your team learn and improve.

## Auto-Created vs Manual Incidents

### Auto-Created Incidents

When a monitor fails or heartbeat is missed, Uplight automatically:
- Creates an incident linked to that monitor/heartbeat
- Sets severity based on the issue
- Sends notifications

When the monitor recovers:
- The incident is automatically updated to "Recovered"
- You still need to manually resolve and close it

### Manual Incidents

You can also create incidents manually for issues detected outside of Uplight:

1. Navigate to **Incidents**
2. Click **Create Incident**
3. Fill in the details
4. Click **Create**

## Notifications

When an incident is created or updated, notifications are sent based on your configuration. See [Notifications](/notifications) for setup details.

## Best Practices

1. **Acknowledge quickly** - Let your team know someone is looking at it
2. **Update status regularly** - Keep the incident status current
3. **Add comments** - Document what you're seeing and doing
4. **Write post-mortems** - Learn from significant incidents
5. **Set appropriate severity** - Don't cry wolf with everything as critical
